About

I study how cities heat up and how design can cool them down, using microclimate simulations, satellite data and machine learning.

I’m an atmospheric scientist and PhD researcher at the University of Kassel with more than eight years of experience across meteorology, remote sensing and urban climate modelling. My work turns large environmental datasets into practical guidance for climate-resilient city planning.

My background runs from physics in Gujarat, through meteorology at KIT and Leibniz Universität Hannover, to my current doctoral research in Germany.

  • Urban climate adaptation
  • Microclimate modelling (ENVI-met)
  • Machine learning for upscaling
  • Remote sensing
  • Heat- and water-sensitive urban development
